## Library landscape (checked, not assumed)

The issue named Clipper2 as the candidate. There is **no `clipper2` package on
PyPI**. What exists:

- `pyclipper` — bindings for Clipper **1**, not Clipper2.
- `pyclipr` 0.1.8 — maintained bindings for **Clipper2 2.0.1**. Chosen.
- `pyclipper2` 0.0.8 — exists but far less established.

Verified before planning that `pyclipr` installs on Windows/Python 3.13 and that
boolean difference correctly yields an outer ring plus an oppositely wound hole,
and that `execute2()` returns a `PolyTreeD` usable for containment.

Also verified up front, rather than discovering it late: **trimesh has no
self-intersection test.** That shaped the validation design.

## Spec issue found

Issue #1 asks for "a V-shaped concave notch with **two** reflex vertices". A
geometrically pure V yields exactly **one** — the apex. Both shoulders are
necessarily convex; making a shoulder reflex would require the material to
overhang the notch.

Caught by the first test written, before any implementation. Resolved by
truncating the apex with a 2 mm flat, which reads as a V and gives two reflex
vertices. Reasoning recorded in `docs/spike-01/geometry-spec.md`.

## Bugs found by tests during the session

Worth recording, because in each case the test caught something a render would
not have.

1. **Hole winding.** Rings are authored CCW, but Clipper2's NonZero fill rule
   needs holes wound opposite. Wrong winding silently *added* the island's area:
   B measured 1030 mm² instead of 966, and the band check reported 64 mm² of
   overlap. No error, no exception — just wrong numbers. Normalisation now lives
   in one place.
2. **Area filtered by a length tolerance.** Cleanup used the 1e-6 mm coordinate
   epsilon to filter slivers by *area*, so 8e-6 mm² of offset round-trip noise
   was reported as a manufacturability defect. Fixed with a separate,
   area-dimensioned threshold.
3. **A wrong test, not wrong code.** The first Clipper2 test summed *absolute*
   ring areas across a difference result and expected 96; the correct figure for
   absolute areas is 104 (100 outer + 4 hole). The adapter was right.

## The finding that matters

Manufacturability cleanup is a morphological opening, and applied naively it
**silently destroys shared boundaries** — it erodes B's copy of the C boundary
while C's own ring stays put. The geometry still renders, still slices, and still
passes every per-body manifold check. The failure is invisible in exactly the
artefacts you would use to look for it.

Cleanup now opens outer rings only and re-cuts holes from authoritative geometry.
Full write-up in `docs/spike-01/conclusion.md`.

## Physical validation (same day, after the software portion)

Andy ran the slicer and print validation.

**Slicer.** All three STL bodies imported into Bambu Studio at the correct shared
position. **No geometry-repair warnings.** Sliced successfully. The manifold
guarantees held up outside our own validation code, and exporting with no
per-body translation proved sufficient for co-registration.

**Print.** Completed successfully. Backing and foreground printed cleanly,
including the concave V-notch and the edge left where the undersized tab was
removed.

**The finding: the island would not fit its zero-clearance opening.**

Mathematically coincident boundaries are correct as canonical artwork geometry
and insufficient as fabrication geometry — coincident surfaces leave no room for
extrusion width, elephant's foot or ordinary print tolerance.

This is a fabrication-geometry finding, not a topology failure, and it vindicated
ADR 0001 decision 6 rather than contradicting it: clearance was already assigned
to export as derived geometry, never to the canonical model. It moved from
hypothetical to required, with a measured reason. That was the decision most
likely to be overturned by printing, and it held.

**Construction model rejected.** Full-depth zero-clearance island insertion is
out. It bundled two problems: no clearance, and asking the island to be a
friction-fit joint when it only needs positioning.

**Direction agreed (not implemented in PR #2).** Continuous backing under the
whole artwork; main colour bodies on the backing; a **shallow registration
recess** in the surrounding body rather than a through-hole; recess larger than
the insert by a configurable clearance; positioning guide rather than friction
fit; pieces glued. Canonical geometry stays exact — clearance is derived at
export.

The cross-section sketch settles more than expected: the recess floor is
main-body material (so the island rests on the red, **not** on the backing), and
**the island stands proud rather than finishing flush**. I had initially assumed
flush and written that into the docs; reading the sketch corrected it. Island
thickness therefore exceeds recess depth, and the artwork gains visible relief at
island boundaries — which puts the current "single flat artwork band" assumption
in the geometry spec up for revision.

Open questions recorded rather than guessed: recess depth as an independent
parameter, where the clearance is applied, whether the recess floor needs its own
minimum-thickness rule, and whether the island bonds to the recess floor rather
than to the backing as Andy's point 6 states.

**Spike 01: PASS.** ADR 0001 decisions 1–7 confirmed; decision 8 revised.

Note: the minimum-feature threshold was *not* physically tested — the tab was
removed before export, so 0.4 mm remains an assumption.
